From a production requirement to a robot design

The design problem of a robot cell is usually written by hand. An engineer turns the production requirement of the plant, the throughput, the parts and their masses, and a plant drawing, into a task specification and a cell model, and then states the optimization problem. The framework hands the writing to language model agents and keeps every design value with the solvers.

In Stage 1 a task agent writes the task specification and cites a page of a manufacturing textbook, retrieved by search, for every derived field. In Stage 2 a work-cell agent names the equipment at every waypoint, and code places it and runs four deterministic checks on units, reach, clearance and sizability before any solver runs. The user confirms each proposal. In Stage 3 an optimization agent chooses from which starts the shared-link coordination below is run.

The design problem

A modular arm is assembled from catalog actuators, brackets, and link tubes cut to length. For a given work cell the designer chooses the two link lengths, the six actuators, and the speed of the approach motion. The two requirements favor opposite link lengths. Reaching the waypoints after they move a little favors longer links, whereas holding the shoulder torque within the rating of a lighter module favors shorter ones.

The two requirements are also evaluated by different kinds of model. Reachability follows from the closed-form inverse kinematics of the arm and has an explicit gradient in the link lengths. The torque and speed demands come from a simulation of the robot program by inverse dynamics and have no gradient at all. A single problem over all variables would have to treat the kinematics as a black box as well.

Shared-link analytical target cascading

The formulation keeps the two disciplines apart. A kinematic subproblem maximizes workspace coverage by a gradient method on a smoothed reach margin. A dynamic subproblem sets the approach speed and selects the actuators that minimize the mass along the trajectory, by a pattern search around the simulation. Each subproblem holds its own copy of the two link lengths, and a system level drives the copies to agreement with augmented-Lagrangian target cascading. Only the link lengths are exchanged. Because coordination is a local procedure, it can be run from three starts that differ in the proportion of the arm, and the optimization agent decides which of them to run.

What the nine cells show

  • The agents write the problem. Before any correction the task agent derives every field correctly in 20 of 45 runs, and the rest are corrected at the confirmation. Retrieval from the textbook raises its accuracy on 38 specification questions from 26 to 79 percent. All 27 runs of the work-cell agent pass the four checks, and in earlier versions of the agent the checks caught 12 of 54 runs that the model could not repair.
  • All 45 coordinated runs converge. One iteration at a zero weight, 13 to 34 otherwise. The kinematic subproblem first returns the longer pair of link lengths in every run, by 138 to 400 mm. At a zero weight a sequential procedure, link lengths first and actuators afterwards, is heavier in six of the nine cells, by up to 2.76 kg.
  • The coordinated designs match an all-in-one solution. In 34 of 36 runs the weighted objective is within one tube step of mass (25 g) of the undecomposed all-in-one solution, at the same number of sizing calls. The other two, in cell (e), settle in a different basin, 52 and 68 g heavier.
  • The start decides the basin, and the agent chooses the starts. A single start from the lightest design misses by up to 2.2 kg. The optimization agent keeps the best of three starts in all 108 runs with 90 percent of their sizing calls, whereas a fixed two-run rule spends 70 percent and misses the basin in four of 36 cases.

One cycle of the robot program

The sizing is done on the robot program the cell actually runs, a sequence of pick-and-place pairs. Each leg is a linear retraction along the tool axis, a joint-space move to the next retract pose, and a linear approach. The part and the parallel-jaw gripper ride on the carrying legs, the gripper alone on the way back, and a part that has been set down stays in the cell as an obstacle, so a pallet is stacked and a shelf fills tier by tier. Where a straight move would sweep through a fixture or a standing part, the program lifts first.

Design studio

Every coordinated design of the paper, on the vendor CAD, in the browser. Pick a work cell and a weight, read the link lengths, the actuators, the mass and the coverage, and play the cycle of the robot program the sizing was done on. The joint trajectories, the waypoints and the fixtures are the ones the figures above were rendered from. A second mode replays the coordination itself, round by round: the kinematic and the dynamic copy of the link lengths on their plane, the inconsistency and the penalty weight, and the two copies standing as stick arms over the final design.
